Araya is a creative technologist, designer, and artist working across interaction, creative coding, and visual design. With a background in public policy and experience building across decentralized systems, their practice explores the structures that shape online life and the culture, intimacy, and awkwardness of being a person on the internet.

Through curiosity and play, they expose how power and visibility move through digital systems and re-imagine alternative ways of engagement. They co-founded (WinPrivacy), a creative agency for non-extractive technologies, framing privacy as a feminist and political project rooted in autonomy, self-expression, and collective power.

Araya can wear many hats, but they're all in the closet because it will ruin their hair.

Glitch Armour reimagines privacy education as a cultural product. We paired a deliberately crafted, high signal web experience with a guerrilla distribution campaign, translating complex security tooling into an intuitive, human-first UX. The project attracted over 10,000 visitors within 48 hours.

Privacy isn't about perfection; it's about power. Drawing from Glitch Feminism, this toolkit helps us weaponize unpredictability, scramble patterns, and refuse to be profiled. A collective act of defiance that turns our data into a source of community strength instead of corporate control.

This project is one of an education initiative of WinPrivacy, a collective that diversifies privacy beyond echo chambers and builds the infrastrctures for a more inclusive privacy movement. It has been in 3 different exhibitions and conferences, spoken to more than 10,000 people.

The toolkit covers 12 categories spanning everyday digital infrastructure: messenger, browser, browser add-ons, search engine, VPN, email, password manager, encrypted storage, file transfer, knowledge system, collaborative editor, and video call. Each category offers curated alternatives evaluated for their privacy practices, giving users a clear path to reclaiming agency over their data without overhauling everything at once.

Research, design, and wrote the printed zine to be distributed all over the world as an alternative method in difussing the information. 300 printed copies sold so far.

We also go on talks at local communities, queer bars, squad houses, conferences for community education surrounding privacy and ethical tech usage.
